Why Fraud and Litigation Checks Matter When Chinese Nationals Set Up Businesses in Malaysia

Collecting a Chinese ID card or passport and verifying the client's identity is an important first step. But is knowing who the client is enough?

ID
Info Data Team
ChinaKYC.my

Malaysia continues to attract Chinese entrepreneurs looking to establish companies, invest or expand their businesses. Many of them rely on Malaysian company secretaries, lawyers and corporate service providers to handle the incorporation process.

For these professionals, collecting a Chinese ID card or passport and verifying the client's identity is an important first step. But is knowing who the client is enough?

A person may have valid identification documents, yet there may be other information about their background that deserves attention.

This is where fraud-related and litigation checks become useful supplementary checks when conducting individual KYC on Chinese nationals.

1

Identity verification alone is not enough

A valid Chinese ID card confirms important identity details, but it does not tell you whether the individual has relevant fraud-related records, current or ongoing cases, previous court cases, or other publicly available legal records.

For company secretaries and lawyers, understanding more about a prospective client's background can help identify matters that may require clarification before accepting an engagement or proceeding with incorporation.

Two areas worth considering are fraud checks and litigation checks.

Fraud checks

Available records that may relate to suspected or reported fraudulent activities.
Litigation checks

Current or ongoing cases, previous court cases and other publicly available legal records.

2

Why fraud checks matter

Fraud checks help identify available records or information associated with an individual that may relate to suspected or reported fraudulent activities.

For example, available records may include:

  • Fraud-related reports or incidents.
  • Records of suspected fraudulent activities.
  • Fraud-related court proceedings.
  • Other relevant records from available sources.

This information may be particularly relevant when a prospective client intends to establish a company involving financial transactions, investments, trading or other commercial activities.

A record does not automatically mean the individual has committed fraud. It should be checked against the client's identity and assessed in context.
